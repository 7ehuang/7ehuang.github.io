We recently visited some tea farm areas and mountains in central Taiwan. In planning this trip, I found articles published by previous travelers particularly helpful (especially posts by [Nick Kembel] (https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/about/)) so I'm documenting some trip details here in case it can be useful to future visitors.


## Summary 
 → means bus, ➤ means high speed rail a.k.a HSR, ⇒ means car

* Day 1: Taipei ➤ Chiayi → Xiding (Accomodation: [SunsweetHouse](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vRXPVk9zYkbvmJiw9))

* Day 2: Xiding → Alishan (Accomodation: [Ying Shan Hotel](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dgq3ouYNsYvYkKFy6))

* Day 3: Alishan → Chiayi ➤ Taichung ⇒ Cingjing (Accomodation: [Misty Villa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qMbbaDHJBu37LaQP9))

* Day 4: Cingjing → Hehuanshan (Accomodation: [Songsyue Lodge](https://maps.app.goo.gl/PvY4zK3ZsuuRysai8). Getting a room here is pretty difficult; you want to try booking 30 days in advance at the right time. See Nick Kembel's [guide](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/songsyue-lodge-hehuanshan/)).

* Day 5: Hehuanshan ⇒ Cingjing (Accomodation: [Cingjing Vienna](https://maps.app.goo.gl/G6RGqgdyGzbMCvWa9))

* Day 6: Cingjing ⇒ Tea farm tour with [Via Tea](https://www.instagram.com/lets_viatea/?hl=en)  ⇒ Taichung ➤ Taipei

## Day 1: Taipei ➤ Chiayi → Xiding
Our first destination was Xiding, which was recommended as a stop before Alishan by [Nick Kembel](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/eryanping-tea-mist-trail-alishan/). From my grandmother's apartment in Taipei, we took an Uber to Taipei Main Station, where we took a high speed rail train departing at 9:01 AM to Chiayi High Speed Rail (HSR) Station (10:28 AM arrival). You can book HSR tickets from Klook at a discount [here](https://www.klook.com/en-CA/activity/73749-thsr-one-way-ticket-taipei/?spm=BookingDetail.ActivityCard&clickId=16b1cebed0).

At Chiayi HSR station, we took the 11:00 AM Bus 7329A to Xiding (arrived ~12:30). More details about taking the bus can be found [here](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/chiayi-to-alishan-bus-schedule/). The same bus goes all the way to Alishan; we just got off early at Xiding. You can either reserve a seat ahead of time for a guaranteed spot (directly from the [bus website](https://alishan.welcometw.com/tour/listAll?category=eoQ&keyword=alishan+bus) or [Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-CA/activity/50411-alishan-round-trip-ticket-alishan-national-forest-recreation-area-ticket/?spm=BookingDetail.ActivityCard&clickId=0ee01f1911)), or just swipe your EasyCard. 

The Xiding bus stop was just a short walk from our accommodation at [SunsweetHouse](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vRXPVk9zYkbvmJiw9), which was super cute and close to the two major hiking trails in the area: Tea Mist Trail and Erayanping Trail.

In between our hikes, we stopped by a nearby [tea shop](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RMPf9ENfP6YQ3BYLA). It looked like a giant mostly empty warehouse where a man was seated at a desk replete with an array of tea serving materials. After some awkward staring, we found the words to ask for a tea tasting and this tipped us into a 1.5 hour long spontaneous and generous tea tasting session in which the tea man shared about his life growing up in Shizhuo and loving tea and conspiracy theories about disappearing scientists and all his past visitors, including some Russian guy with whom he conversed with completely via Google Translate.

For an extra fee, Sunsweet provides a plentiful hot pot dinner, complemented by Winter Melon slushie, house oolong tea, and grapefruit tea.

## Day 2: Xiding → Alishan
We woke up early (~4:50 AM) in attempts to capture the sunrise but it was a bit too cloudy. It was still a nice and peaceful atmosphere on the viewing platform in front of the [SunsweetHouse](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vRXPVk9zYkbvmJiw9) lobby though.

We thoroughly enjoyed the [SunsweetHouse](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vRXPVk9zYkbvmJiw9) complimentary buffet breakfast. Some highlights included tea jelly, mini sandwiches, congee, french toast sticks, and fried tea leaves. It was also a marvel that they had someone making pour over coffee en masse for all the hotel guests, a fact that encapsulates the luxurious but cozy atmosphere at Sunsweet. 

After checking out, we took the 7322 bus to Alishan (some time after 9AM; simply checked Google Maps for upcoming bus times). The bus arrived a little later than the time noted on Google Maps (maybe 15-20 minutes) but luckily it worked out ... Again, we swiped our EasyCard for this ride. There were a lot of foreign tourists on this bus and there was a brief 10 minute rest stop (I was super freaked out that the bus would leave me behind when I went to the bathroom here).

Taking the bus to Alishan gives you a slight discount on the park entry fee. Our hotel for this night ([Ying Shan Hotel](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dgq3ouYNsYvYkKFy6)) was inside the park. The location of this hotel was convenient, but after the cutesy Sunsweet House vibes, [Ying Shan Hotel](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dgq3ouYNsYvYkKFy6) felt a bit sterile (but that's ok).

You can take a small bus or train from Alishan service station into the main park area, but after dropping off our bags at [Ying Shan Hotel](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dgq3ouYNsYvYkKFy6) we just walked 20 minutes into the park. We finished walking through most of the park in around 1.5-2 hours (including a lunch break featuring bamboo/tofu soup, warmed chestnuts, tea eggs, and Taiwanese sausage wrapped in rice). We then embarked on the hubristic enterprise of hiking Tamshan trail, which partially followed an abandoned railway and consisted of what felt like constantly ascending stairs. This unexpectedly challenging hike left us famished for dinner. We abandoned attempts to get a spot at one of the restaurants (too many large groups) and ended up ordering inhaling some dishes at some food stalls in the park instead.

## Day 3: Alishan → Chiayi ➤ Taichung ⇒ Cingjing 
This was the biggest travel day, but we had some fun stops as well.

[Ying Shan Hotel](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dgq3ouYNsYvYkKFy6) does not have breakfast on site, but provides vouchers for breakfast at a nearby [restaurant](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mC9BS4fWjRqErg2DA). There were a few restaurants in the area that offered very similar looking cafeteria-styled breakfasts, featuring congee, hot soy milk, 3-in-1 coffee, tea, noodles, toast, coconut jam, etc. After breakfast, we wandered around some shops and ended up spending an extended period of time at [Honest Charlie, Honest Tea](https://maps.app.goo.gl/LjpKFWxuL35yR7DX6) tea shop where the shopowner kindly treated us to a tea tasting session. Her extollations of tea drinking culture and detailing of her days selling tea and hiking the nearby mountains were truly inspiring and I thoroughly enjoyed our conversation (I'm always flattered when people claim that I sound Taiwanese even though I know that's patently false especially if we reach conversational topics beyond 2nd grade level ...). But seriously, her teas are divine, and she sells Alishan coffee as well. Please pay her a visit!

Because we were going from the first to last bus stop (and because missing this bus would screw up the travel plans for the rest of the day), we reserved spots on [Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-CA/activity/50411-alishan-round-trip-ticket-alishan-national-forest-recreation-area-ticket/?spm=BookingDetail.ActivityCard&clickId=0ee01f1911) for the 10:10 AM Bus 7329 from Alishan to Chiayi HSR. We took the 14:08 Chiayi HSR to Taichung, which arrived at 14:30 to be in time for our 15:00 (3pm) pickup ([Klook private transfer from Taichung to Cingjing](https://www.klook.com/en-CA/activity/19127-shared-transfer-service-cingjing-taichung/?spm=BookingDetail.ActivityCard&clickId=78f722c8cd)). The driver took us directly to our accomodation at [Misty Villa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qMbbaDHJBu37LaQP9). 

I had warm sentiments towards [Misty Villa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qMbbaDHJBu37LaQP9) even before getting there because I had been getting very cute LINE messages from the manager after booking (you DM them on LINE to make the reservation) featuring cute stickers as useful information about our stay was conveyed (i.e. temperature info, dinner options, pictures of the rooms, etc.). We were warmly welcomed by the BnB manager and she even upgraded our room for free. We paid an extra fee for dinner and it was worth every last NTD. The meal was five courses and ended with a local specialty: some kind of tree grape??

## Day 4: Cingjing → Hehuanshan
Our plan for this day was to travel to Hehuanshan National Park area for some hiking. There are only 3 buses a day from Cingjing to Hehuanshan and each bus can only hold ~20 people, so getting a spot is challenging. You can reserve bus tickets starting 7 days in advance [here](https://ntbus.welcometw.com/tour/listAll?category=5bM) (tickets drop at approximately midnight Taiwan time but sometimes you have to wait 10-15 minutes after midnight for the tickets to appear ...). Here is Nick Kembel's [guide](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/hehuanshan-sunrise-hiking-snow/#From_Cingjing_Farm). 

I only succeeded in acquiring one 8:20 AM ticket in advance but we decided to take the risk and see if the bus driver would let an additional person onto the bus. Luckily he had ONE free spot so we were able to get up to Songsyue Lodge by ~9:20 AM. The [Misty Villa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/qMbbaDHJBu37LaQP9) manager very kindly arranged for us to have a packed breakfast meal (sandwiches and boiled eggs) very last minute when I told her we were going to try to take the 8:20 AM bus the same morning (usually a breakfast buffet is offered 8-9AM but we would've missed it with our schedule). 

Thematically consistent with booking the Hehuanshan bus tickets, getting a reservation at Songsyue Lodge (the only hotel in the Hehuanshan park) is notoriously difficult. Nick Kembel's [guide](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/songsyue-lodge-hehuanshan/) for snagging a spot was an indispensable resource: XX Basically, you need to log on at exactly the right time 30 days in advance of your anticipated visit. Also important note!!! Songsyue Lodge does not provide towels so you should bring your own (or sacrifice a shirt if you didn't realize this beforehand ...)

## Day 5: Hehuanshan ⇒ Cingjing
We finally succeeded in seeing a sunrise today! The sun emerged like a brilliant yolk between two layers of clouds. 

After hiking Mt. Shimen and Xiao Qi Lai, we planned to take the noon bus back to Qingjing but near the end of the Xiao Qi Lai hike, we met a lovely couple from Kaoshiong who offered to drive us down the mountain. After eating lunch (and hiding from the rainstorm), we dropped off our bags at [Cingjing Vienna](https://maps.app.goo.gl/G6RGqgdyGzbMCvWa9) and then wandered around a bit aimlessly under some dismal grey clouds. Adventures included a visit to Swiss Garden (mediocre, "this place would have hit when I was like 6") and Skywalk (saw some sheep in the fog). Dinner was at this extremely [hole in the wall place](https://maps.app.goo.gl/XbY6eifrAJr3BKgRA) (it actually looked like part of someone's home; you have to walk down an alley to find it) a few minutes walking distance from our accommodation - seems like it's basically one person cooking everything?? We definitely ordered too much food but the temptation to try all the dishes was too overwhelming...

## Day 6: Cingjing ⇒ Tea farm tour with [Via Tea](https://www.instagram.com/lets_viatea/?hl=en)  ⇒ Taichung ➤ Taipei
Months ago, I signed up for a tea farm tour with [lets_viatea](https://www.instagram.com/lets_viatea/?hl=en), which is run by a PhD student Rachel Lee (see her recent work [here](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0743016725004383)). Rachel and her friend Wayne drove us to a local tea farm from our BnB [Cingjing Vienna](https://maps.app.goo.gl/G6RGqgdyGzbMCvWa9), where we learned so much about organic tea farming, social policies governing tea farming practices, social dynamics among tea farmers, different kind of teas, etc. etc. After the tea farm tour, tea tasting session, delicious noodle lunch, and farm exploration, Wayne drove us to the farmer's house in Puli where we met the couple that owns the tea farm that we visited in the morning. The whole day was such a special and unique experience!

From Puli, Wayne drove us to Taichung HSR, where we said a sad goodbye before catching a HSR to Taipei. Unlike our previous HSR journeys, we didn't manage to get reserve tickets (in which you are assigned a seat) because we wanted the scheduling flexibility ... but even with the non-reserve tickets (which come at a discount) we managed to snag seats and make it back to grandma's apartment in Taipei before 7:00pm.
